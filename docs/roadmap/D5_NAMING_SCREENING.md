# D5 — Naming screening (issue #35)

**Round 2, 17 November 2026.** Preliminary screening from public sources, **not trademark clearance, legal advice or a guarantee of availability.** **No name is selected and none has been applied.** Tyler rejected round 1's recommendation, **Cartouche**, and chooses the name ([#35](https://github.com/tylerbarnard23-PM/atlas-proposal-engine/issues/35)); the product still ships as "Atlas Proposal Engine". Round 1 is kept below as a superseded record.

## The reset brief

- Professional advisor and wealth software.
- **Easy to pronounce, spell and remember after hearing it once.**
- Familiar words, clear compounds or natural-sounding brand names. No obscure historical terms, awkward spellings or a forced cartography theme.
- AI is a feature of the workspace, not the identity.
- Pronunciation and unfortunate sound-alikes checked explicitly.
- Fit and clarity come first; **domain availability is a separate check.**

**Why rename at all:** the nearest products to this one are Telescope Atlas, Meotis Finance Atlas and Eton Solutions' AtlasFive (from #35).

## Recommendation

**Clearpage** — "Clearpage: Advisor Proposal Workspace."

- **Say it, spell it, remember it.** Two everyday words said exactly as written (*KLEER-payj*). Nobody asks "how do you spell that?"
- **Fit.** The product's output is a proposal page, and its promise is clarity: each value shows its source and date, the scope and denominator are stated, and drafts stay clearly marked until the advisor approves. "Clear" says that without claiming a result.
- **Claims.** Nothing about trading, performance, guarantees or fairness, and nothing about AI. The AI assistant remains a feature the descriptor and product copy can mention.
- **Collisions.** No wealth, advice or software product or adviser of this exact name was found. Exact-name GitHub matches are few (22 repositories, all small, such as a browser extension and a new-tab page). Practical `.com` options were unregistered when checked (below).
- **The honest catch: Clearpage sounds like Clearpay**, the buy-now-pay-later brand Afterpay uses in the UK and parts of Europe. A listener could mishear one as the other. It is a different field (consumer payments), and "page" is a different word from "pay", but the risk is real and should be weighed by the owner and counsel. Clearpage is also a LaTeX command name, which matters only to developers.

**Runner-up: Fairdraft** — no collisions of its own and the most distinctive (4 GitHub repositories), but it sits next to an advisor-technology company called **Draft Technologies** (Draft Insights), and "fair" could be read as a claim of fair treatment. Details below.

## Method and limits (round 2)

- **Candidates:** familiar words and plain compounds, generated against the brief above; 12 were screened mechanically (domains, GitHub, npm) and by web search; four are finalists.
- **Web:** the session's web-search tool, exact-name queries combined with wealth, advisor, RIA, software and platform terms. A miss is weak evidence. Several results are third-party directories whose figures can be stale.
- **Trademarks were not screened in round 2** (the brief asked for current uses, exact-name matches and domain options). **Not checked:** the USPTO's TSDR and the state, EU, WIPO and UK registers; common-law use; other languages.
- **Domains:** the registries' own RDAP services (Verisign for `.com`, Identity Digital for `.ai`, Google Registry for `.app` and `.dev`) plus DNS. `.io` has no RDAP service in the IANA bootstrap, so a name server is read as "registered". "Not found" means unregistered **at the moment checked**; it reserves nothing and can change.
- **Handles:** GitHub only. X, LinkedIn, Instagram and the like were not checked.
- **Pronunciation and sound-alikes** are my reading of the spelling and are not a test with listeners. Say each spoken introduction aloud, ideally to someone who hasn't seen the name.
- Nothing was bought, registered or reserved. No settings or accounts were touched.

## Decision gate

### Round 1 recommendation (rejected)

**Cartouche — "Cartouche: Advisor Proposal Workspace."** Pronounced *kar-TOOSH*.

- **What it means.** On a map, the cartouche is the framed block that says what the sheet is: its title, scale, legend and sources. This product does exactly that job for a proposal: every value carries its source and date, the scope and its denominator are stated, the readiness checklist is visible and the document says what it is and is not. A framed, titled, documented page is also the product's output.
- **Continuity.** It keeps the cartography lineage of "Atlas", so the project's history and screenshots still read as the same thing, and "formerly Atlas Proposal Engine" is a natural note.
- **Claims.** It suggests no trading, no execution, no performance and no guaranteed outcome, and no capability the product lacks. The AI is a feature of the workspace, not part of the name, which keeps the "optional AI assistance" description honest.
- **Collisions.** Of the five names screened, it showed the fewest in wealth, advice and software: no advice firm, no software vendor and no software-class trademark in the checks below. Its exact name is used by an unrelated Swift game-launcher project on GitHub, which doesn't collide with this field.
- **Cost of choosing it.** The spelling and pronunciation are French, so some people will hesitate on them. Its single-word domains are all taken, though several practical alternatives were unregistered when checked (below).

**Runner-up: Theodolite** — a precision instrument for measuring angles, which suits the engine-checked figures, but it is longer, harder to spell, and a registered software-class trademark for a navigation and surveying app exists (below). If you prefer an unmistakable, less foreign word, choose it over the others.

### Method and limits

- **Name criteria:** distinctive; suits an advisor proposal workspace with optional AI; no suggestion of trading, execution, guaranteed performance or absent capabilities.
- **Web:** the session's web-search tool. Exact-name queries combined with fintech, wealth, investment, advisor, software and trademark terms. Coverage is limited and a miss proves little.

- **Handles:** GitHub user and organization names, observed through the public API. **Not checked:** X, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube, Bluesky and the like, which can't be observed reliably without accounts. Treat those as uncertain.
- **Repository evidence:** GitHub's public search counts and top results on 17 November 2026; counts shift.
- Nothing was bought, registered or reserved. No settings or accounts were touched.
